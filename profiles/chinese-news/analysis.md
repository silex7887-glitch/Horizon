# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a Chinese-news item gives readers timely, credible information about a political, social, or technology event that matters to people in the Chinese-speaking world.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** A change of policy or leadership, a major security incident, or a technology result with wide consequences for China or the region.
- **7-8: Important.** A concrete policy step, diplomatic or security development, corporate event, or research result that changes what readers need to know.
- **5-6: Useful.** Credible reporting that clarifies who did what and why it matters, but with limited new consequences.
- **3-4: Low value.** Routine statements, incremental commentary, or developments already widely known without a new fact.
- **0-2: Noise.** Rumors, unsourced claims, slogans, or promotional copy with no checkable event.

# Evaluation guidance

Weigh the scale of the event, the credibility of the source, and timeliness. Distinguish reported facts from allegations, official claims, and opinion. Do not treat dramatic language or unverified figures as importance. A Chinese-language technology item can score in the same range as a political item when the result is concrete and sourced.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
