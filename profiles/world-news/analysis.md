# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a world-affairs item gives readers timely, credible information about a political, diplomatic, or security event that changes the situation for a country, institution, or civilian population.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** Outbreak or major expansion of armed conflict, a ceasefire or peace agreement, a change of government, sweeping sanctions, or a diplomatic break with wide consequences.
- **7-8: Important.** A concrete escalation, election result, treaty step, casualty or displacement figure, or policy decision that materially changes a conflict or a bilateral relationship.
- **5-6: Useful.** Credible reporting that clarifies who did what, where, and with what immediate effect, but with limited new consequences.
- **3-4: Low value.** Routine statements, incremental commentary, or developments already widely known without a new fact.
- **0-2: Noise.** Rumors, unsourced claims, partisan slogans, or content with no checkable event.

# Evaluation guidance

Weigh the scale of the event, the credibility of the source, timeliness, and whether the item states actors, locations, and outcomes. Distinguish reported facts from allegations, official claims, and opinion. Do not treat dramatic language or casualty figures as importance unless the source supports them. Do not reward partisan framing.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
